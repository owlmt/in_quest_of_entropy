# In quest of entropy: where the entropy claims break

Entropy is the load-bearing assumption of modern cryptography, yet nearly every concrete entropy claim in deployed systems has been shown — by peer-reviewed research, CVEs, or both — to be **either the wrong measure, mis-estimated, or applied to a source that never had the entropy it promised**. This post enhances the original "In Quest of Entropy" taxonomy by separating two perpendicular axes — the *mathematical* measures (Shannon, min-, collision-, Rényi, conditional, joint, KL, guessing, smooth-min, von Neumann) and the three *engineering* layers (physical → system → cryptographic) — and, for every node, points to the strongest published research showing the claim is broken.

The picture that emerges is sharp. **Pure mathematical entropy measures are almost never "broken" in themselves**; what breaks is (a) the *modeling choice* (Shannon used where min-entropy is required), (b) the *estimator* (NIST SP 800-90B's ten estimators both under- and over-estimate on realistic sources), or (c) the *physical/logical source* (boot-time RNG holes, RDRAND microarchitectural leaks, Dual_EC backdoors, ECDSA nonce reuse). The catastrophic failures in the field — Debian OpenSSL CVE-2008-0166, *Mining Your Ps and Qs*, PS3 ECDSA, KRACK, ROCA, DUHK, Minerva, TPM-FAIL, Juniper Dual_EC — all live in this third category. Understanding which kind of break you're looking at is the single most useful diagnostic when reading any "high-entropy" security claim.

## A unified taxonomy: measures × layers

The Rényi family unifies the mathematical measures. For a discrete distribution, **H_α(X) = (1/(1−α)) · log Σ p_iᵅ**, with four limit/special cases that dominate cryptography:

| α | Measure | Crypto role | Where it breaks in practice |
|---|---|---|---|
| α → 0 | **Hartley / max-entropy** H_0 = log\|supp\| | Upper bound on keyspace | Vacuous when distribution is concentrated (Bonneau 2012) |
| α → 1 | **Shannon** H_1 = −Σ p log p | Information theory, malware/anomaly detection | Wrong measure for cryptographic seeding (Cachin 1997; DORS 2008); evadable as a packer signal (Mantovani NDSS 2020) |
| α = 2 | **Collision / Rényi-2** H_2 = −log Σ p_i² | Leftover Hash Lemma, privacy amplification | Sample-efficient to estimate but loose vs. min-entropy; LRS estimator overestimates (Kim-Guyot-Kim 2023) |
| α → ∞ | **Min-entropy** H_∞ = −log max p_i | NIST SP 800-90B; the only sound seed measure | The measure is correct; deployed sources lacked it (Heninger USENIX 2012) |

The ordering **H_0 ≥ H_1 ≥ H_2 ≥ H_∞** is the reason min-entropy is conservative and Shannon is dangerous: a distribution can have Shannon entropy ≈ ½ log L while min-entropy is 1 bit (DORS' canonical "half-uniform-half-delta" counterexample). The **leftover hash lemma** (Impagliazzo-Levin-Luby STOC 1989; tightened in Barak-Dodis-Krawczyk et al. CRYPTO 2011) requires *min-entropy or collision entropy* — Shannon is not enough — and inherently loses ≥ 2 log(1/ε) bits. Cachin's 1997 ETH thesis ("Entropy Measures and Unconditional Security in Cryptography"), Barak-Shaltiel-Tromer (CHES 2003), and Dodis-Ostrovsky-Reyzin-Smith (Eurocrypt 2004 / SIAM JC 2008) are the seminal sources establishing min-entropy as the right notion for cryptographic seeding.

These mathematical measures connect to the **three engineering layers** as follows: physical sources (Layer 1) are modeled by stochastic processes whose **min-entropy per sample** is the quantity NIST SP 800-90B tries to estimate; system sources (Layer 2) accumulate min-entropy via a *robust extractor* (the model of Barak-Halevi 2005 and Dodis-Pointcheval-Ruhault-Vergnaud-Wichs 2013); cryptographic sources (Layer 3) consume that extracted min-entropy as seeds, keys, nonces, and IVs whose security proofs assume uniform randomness. A break at any layer cascades upward.

## Mathematical measures and where each breaks

### Shannon entropy — the wrong tool, weaponized as a malware signal

The seminal "broken claim" is not against Shannon entropy as mathematics but against its **application as a cryptographic strength metric**. Skórski's *Shannon Entropy versus Rényi Entropy from a Cryptographic Viewpoint* (IACR ePrint 2014/967) [1] gives tight gap bounds showing distributions with near-maximal Shannon entropy can have trivially small min-entropy. The same point is made operationally by **DORS** (Eurocrypt 2004) [2] with explicit counterexamples where any deterministic extractor must fail.

In malware analysis, **Lyda & Hamrock** (IEEE S&P 2007) [3] established the Shannon-entropy ≥ ~7.0-bits/byte threshold for detecting packed/encrypted code. This is now systematically evadable: **Mantovani et al.**, *Prevalence and Impact of Low-Entropy Packing Schemes in the Malware Ecosystem* (NDSS 2020) [4], found >30% of malware in a 50k-sample corpus uses low-entropy packing tricks (XOR with patterns, base64-style encodings); **Aghakhani et al.**, *When Malware is Packin' Heat* (NDSS 2020) [5], showed ML classifiers built on byte entropy detect *packer identity*, not maliciousness; and **Menéndez et al.** (*Expert Sys. Appl.* 2019; *Entropy* 2021) built EEE, an evolutionary polymorphic packer that controls entropy shape and drives detector false-negative rates to 100%.

### Min-entropy and the NIST SP 800-90B estimators

The measure is sound; the **estimators are not**. NIST SP 800-90B (Jan 2018) takes the *minimum* of 10 estimators (Most-Common-Value, Collision, Markov, Compression, t-tuple, LRS, MultiMCW, Lag, MultiMMC, LZ78Y), an aggregation that *amplifies* any single estimator's underestimation. **Kelsey-McKay-Sönmez Turan**, *Predictive Models for Min-Entropy Estimation* (CHES 2015, ePrint 2015/600) [6], introduced the predictor-based estimators while observing the predecessors *underestimated Random.org* by ~3 bits and that LZ78Y returns physically impossible values (>11 bits/sample) on mismatched sources. **Zhu, Lin, Ma, Jing, Zhuang**, *Analysis and Improvement of Entropy Estimators in NIST SP 800-90B for Non-IID Sources* (IACR ToSC 2017/3), identified systematic Collision/Compression underestimation. **Kim, Guyot, Kim**, *Generalized LRS Estimator for Min-Entropy Estimation* (IEEE TIFS 2023, arXiv:2112.09376), showed LRS structurally estimates *collision entropy* — an upper bound on min-entropy — so it overestimates. The 2025 *Cryptography and Communications* paper *Observations on NIST SP 800-90B entropy estimators* (DOI 10.1007/s12095-025-00778-7) confirms the joint pathology: estimators are correlated, so the "take-the-min" defense is weaker than designed.

Crucially, the formal **robustness** of the consuming extractor is also broken: **Dodis-Pointcheval-Ruhault-Vergnaud-Wichs**, */dev/random is not Robust* (CCS 2013, ePrint 2013/338) [7], prove Linux's RNG cannot recover from state compromise even with ample fresh entropy injection. The companion **Dodis-Shamir-Stephens-Davidowitz-Wichs**, *How to Eat Your Entropy and Have it Too* (Crypto 2014) [8], formalizes the "premature next" attack.

### Collision entropy, Rényi general family, and Hartley

Collision entropy underpins the Bennett-Brassard-Crépeau-Maurer privacy amplification (IEEE TIT 1995) — they showed *collision* entropy, not Shannon, governs universal hashing. The measure itself is sound; failures are again estimator-level. **Acharya-Orlitsky-Suresh-Tyagi**, *The Complexity of Estimating Rényi Entropy* (IEEE TIT 63(1), 2017) [9], proved Rényi estimation has sample complexity Θ(k^{1−1/α}) for integer α>1 (sublinear for α=2) but *super-linear* for α<1, making low-α regimes (closer to Shannon) harder to estimate than Shannon itself. **Skórski**, *Renyi Entropy Estimation Revisited* (APPROX/RANDOM 2017) and *On the Complexity of Estimating Renyi Divergences* (arXiv:1702.01666) [10], shows divergence estimation is unbounded in general. **Mironov**, *Rényi Differential Privacy* (CSF 2017) [11], is not a break but uses Rényi divergence to *tighten* DP composition, exposing how loose prior (ε,δ)-DP analyses of DP-SGD were.

**Hartley/max-entropy** is essentially log of support size; no published work breaks H_0 itself because there is nothing to break. Its operational hazard is the universal "the keyspace is 2^k, therefore secure" fallacy demolished by **Bonneau**, *The Science of Guessing* (IEEE S&P 2012) [12], which showed Yahoo's 70M-password corpus has effective guessing entropy ~10 bits despite enormous H_0.

### Conditional entropy and joint entropy in side-channel and QIF

For side-channels, **Standaert-Malkin-Yung**, *A Unified Framework for the Analysis of Side-Channel Key Recovery Attacks* (Eurocrypt 2009) [13], tied conditional entropy / mutual information to success rate and guessing entropy; **Gierlichs et al.**, *Mutual Information Analysis* (CHES 2008) [14], made MIA a generic distinguisher. Both are well-defined; the *finite-sample estimation* breaks. **Prouff & Rivain** (ACNS 2009) and **Veyrat-Charvillon & Standaert**, *Mutual Information Analysis: How, When and Why?* (CHES 2009), demonstrated that MIA's apparent advantage disappears in practical regimes and that the Eurocrypt-2009 MI metric and the MIA distinguisher are inequivalent in finite samples. **Renauld-Standaert-Veyrat-Charvillon**, *Algebraic Side-Channel Attacks on the AES* (CHES 2009) [15], and **Veyrat-Charvillon-Gérard-Standaert**, *Soft Analytical Side-Channel Attacks* (Asiacrypt 2014), show per-instant H(X|Y) massively underestimates leakage when cross-sample algebraic dependencies are exploited.

For quantitative information flow, **Geoffrey Smith**, *On the Foundations of Quantitative Information Flow* (FoSSaCS 2009) [16], is the seminal "broken" result: programs with low Shannon-based mutual information leak can be one-guess vulnerable. **Köpf & Smith** (CSF 2010) showed Shannon-bounded timing leakage for blinded RSA is exponentially loose vs. the true min-entropy vulnerability. **Alvim-Chatzikokolakis-Palamidessi** (ESORICS 2011) generalized this to g-leakage, exhibiting concrete inversions of Shannon-MI orderings under realistic adversaries; **Palamidessi-Alvim-Andrés** (ARSPA-WITS 2010) [17] showed Shannon-MI fails outright for interactive systems.

### KL divergence and the demise of entropy-based anomaly detection

Entropy-based traffic anomaly detection, popularized by **Lakhina-Crovella-Diot** (SIGCOMM 2005) [18], has been comprehensively undermined. **Nychis-Sekar-Andersen-Kim-Zhang**, *An Empirical Evaluation of Entropy-Based Traffic Anomaly Detection* (IMC 2008) [19], found port and address entropies are so correlated they yield essentially one signal, and that the resulting detectors miss scanning/flooding attacks. **Brauckhoff-Salamatian-May** (INFOCOM 2009) showed PCA-based KL detectors flip normal/anomalous subspaces under modest sampling changes. **Rubinstein et al.**, *ANTIDOTE* (IMC 2009) [20], demonstrates **poisoning**: modest chaff during training shifts the detector so subsequent DoS attacks fall below threshold. The canonical evasion result is **Wagner & Soto**, *Mimicry Attacks on Host-Based IDS* (CCS 2002) [21]: craft intrusions with zero KL divergence from the trained distribution.

### Guessing entropy — incomparable with Shannon

**Massey**, *Guessing and Entropy* (ISIT 1994) [22], introduced G(X) = Σ i·p_i and showed Shannon entropy is a poor lower bound on guessing complexity. **Pliam**, *On the Incomparability of Entropy and Marginal Guesswork* (Indocrypt 2000), proved that for any separation m, distributions exist where Shannon and guessing entropy differ from the actual α-success guesswork by ≥ m bits. **Bonneau** (S&P 2012) [12] operationalized this against passwords: NIST SP800-63 Shannon estimates are unrelated to crack-resistance; he proposed α-guesswork μ_α and showed passwords give <10 bits online / ~20 bits offline. **Weir-Aggarwal-Collins-Stern**, *Testing Metrics for Password Creation Policies* (CCS 2010) [23], empirically demolished NIST SP800-63 by cracking 32M+ passwords. In side-channel work, **Veyrat-Charvillon-Gérard-Standaert**, *Security Evaluations Beyond Computing Power* (Eurocrypt 2013) [24], showed per-subkey guessing entropies do not combine — prior side-channel claims underestimated full-key cost by 2^50 or more.

### Smooth min-entropy and von Neumann entropy in QKD

Smooth min-entropy was introduced by **Renner**'s thesis *Security of Quantum Key Distribution* (ETH 2005, arXiv:quant-ph/0512258) [25] as the right one-shot quantity for randomness extraction with quantum side information. The measure is not broken, but its bounds were repeatedly shown to be **too loose for practical block sizes**: **Tomamichel-Lim-Gisin-Renner**, *Tight Finite-Key Analysis for Quantum Cryptography* (Nature Comm. 2012), proved the Scarani-Renner finite-key bounds required 10^10–10^12-signal blocks for security and gave much tighter direct bounds; **Bratzik et al.** (Phys. Rev. A 2011) [26] showed earlier smooth-min-entropy bounds gave *zero* secure rate at realistic short block sizes.

Von Neumann entropy S(ρ) = −Tr(ρ log ρ) appears in the Devetak-Winter key-rate formula. The measure is mathematically sound; what breaks is the **physical-assumption layer** of the security proof. **Brassard-Lütkenhaus-Mor-Sanders**, *Limitations on Practical Quantum Cryptography* (PRL 2000), showed photon-number-splitting completely defeats BB84 proofs that assume single-photon sources — motivating decoy states. **Lydersen et al.**, *Hacking Commercial Quantum Cryptography Systems by Tailored Bright Illumination* (Nature Photonics 4:686, 2010) [27], blinds APDs with bright light and forces attacker-chosen clicks with zero QBER — full key recovery despite a valid von Neumann bound. **Gerhardt et al.**, *Full-field Implementation of a Perfect Eavesdropper on a Quantum Cryptography System* (Nature Comm. 2:349, 2011) [28], demonstrated end-to-end key extraction over 290m fiber with no monitored parameter showing anomaly. The conclusion is uniform: when the measurement/state model is violated, S(ρ) bounds the wrong distribution.

## Layer 1: physical entropy sources, broken at the silicon

**Ring-oscillator TRNGs.** **Markettos & Moore**, *The Frequency Injection Attack on Ring-Oscillator-Based True Random Number Generators* (CHES 2009) [29], is the canonical break: a sinusoidal signal on the power supply injection-locks the two ROs of a secure microcontroller TRNG, collapsing its effective keyspace from 2^64 to ~3,300 and breaking a 2004 EMV "Chip and PIN" card with only 13 attempts. **Bayon et al.**, *Contactless Electromagnetic Active Attack* (COSADE 2012) [30], achieves the same effect via near-field EM with no contact, on a 50-RO Wold TRNG. **Osuka et al.** (IEEE T-EMC 2019) and **Fujimoto et al.** (2022) extend this to TERO designs, and **Madau et al.** [31] show pulsed EMFI degrades RO delay-chain TRNGs.

**Quantum RNGs.** **Hurley-Smith & Hernandez-Castro**, *Quantum Leap and Crash* (ACM TOPS 2020) [32], found significant biases in IDQ Quantis 4M/16M/USB, Comscire PQ32MU, ANU Online QRNG, and Humboldt-Physik raw streams that **pass NIST SP800-22 yet fail Dieharder/Ent/TestU01** — directly indicting the standardized certification suites. **Smith et al.**, *Out-of-Band Electromagnetic Injection Attack on a Photonic QRNG* (Phys. Rev. Applied 15:044044, 2021) [33], achieves attacker-chosen output via antenna injection that still passes statistical tests. **Ponosova et al.** [34] (PLoS ONE 2020, arXiv:1910.08152) bias an IDQ Clavis2 by injecting light through ventilation openings. **Petrov et al.** (arXiv:2004.04996) [35] independently assessed Quantis and identified non-random contributions from electronics imperfections and internal post-processing; the 2025 paper *Statistical Invisibility of a Physical Attack on QRNGs After Randomness Extraction* (arXiv:2508.21498) [36] demonstrates power-supply-ripple attacks that survive randomness extraction unseen by SP800-22.

**Thermal/avalanche and Intel/AMD CPU RNGs.** **Becker-Regazzoni-Paar-Burleson**, *Stealthy Dopant-Level Hardware Trojans* (CHES 2013) [37], modifies dopant polarity in the post-processing of Intel Ivy Bridge's RNG to fix the effective entropy to *n* attacker-chosen bits while passing Intel BIST, NIST SP800-22, and optical reverse-engineering. **CVE-2025-62626 (AMD-SB-7055)**, *AMD Zen 5 RDSEED Returns All-Zero Silently* (disclosed Nov 2025, Gregory Price/Meta) [38], is the most recent: 16-/32-bit RDSEED returns zero >10% of the time while setting CF=1 (success); Linux now disables RDSEED entirely on affected platforms. **CrossTalk / SRBDS (CVE-2020-0543)** by **Ragab et al.** (IEEE S&P 2021) [39] shows RDRAND/RDSEED route through a cross-core staging buffer that allows transient-execution reads from another core, enabling full ECDSA-from-SGX extraction. The 2019 AMD Ryzen 3000 microcode bug (RDRAND returning all-ones) [40] is the field's reminder that vendor entropy instructions can silently fail.

**Metastability and chaos.** *True random number generator resistant to frequency injection attacks* (Electronics Letters 51(13), 2015) [41] demonstrates metastability TRNGs are sensitive to frequency injection. **Álvarez & Li**, *Some Basic Cryptographic Requirements for Chaos-Based Cryptosystems* (Int. J. Bifurcation Chaos 16(8), 2006) [42], is the foundational catalog showing dynamical degradation of digital chaotic maps (finite-precision orbit collapse, periodicity) and trivial chosen-plaintext breaks of nearly every published "chaos cipher."

**PUFs as entropy.** **Rührmair et al.**, *Modeling Attacks on Physical Unclonable Functions* (CCS 2010) [43], uses logistic regression / evolution strategies to build software clones of Arbiter, RO, XOR-Arbiter, Lightweight-Secure, and Feed-Forward PUFs to near-perfect prediction from observed CRPs. **Becker**, *The Gap Between Promise and Reality* (CHES 2015) [44], exploits reliability information to break XOR-Arbiter PUFs once thought secure against CRP modeling. **Tajik et al.**, *Photonic Side-Channel Analysis of Arbiter PUFs* (J. Crypto 2017), uses photon-emission microscopy to extract physical delays.

**Photon detection and radioactive decay.** Stipčević (arXiv:1103.4381) [45] catalogs detector dead-time, afterpulsing, twilight detections, and imperfect beam-splitter bias as universal sources of non-randomness in photonic QRNGs. **Tamura & Shikano**, *Secure random number generation from parity-symmetric radiations* (Nature Comm. Physics 5:65, 2022) [46], explicitly states the security of radioactive-decay timing RNGs has *never been proven* because the timing-based protocol relies on hard-to-control phenomenology; they propose a parity-based reformulation that *can* be proven.

## Layer 2: system entropy, broken at the OS

**Linux /dev/random.** **Gutterman-Pinkas-Reinman**, *Analysis of the Linux Random Number Generator* (IEEE S&P 2006) [47], reverse-engineered kernel 2.6.10 and presented forward-prediction and entropy-starvation attacks. **Lacharme-Röck-Strubel-Videau**, *The Linux Pseudorandom Number Generator Revisited* (IACR ePrint 2012/251) [48], gave the first accurate post-Gutterman mathematical description and showed the entropy estimator is unreliable. **Dodis-Pointcheval-Ruhault-Vergnaud-Wichs**, */dev/random is not Robust* (CCS 2013) [7], proved formal non-robustness. **Cornejo & Ruhault** (CCS 2014) extended this to partial state corruption. **Jason A. Donenfeld**'s 2022 BLAKE2s rework (Linux 5.17/5.18) [49] eliminated SHA-1 mixing and the 4096-bit LFSR pool — and was briefly reverted on some architectures because jitter-entropy bootstrap fails on m68k/microblaze/sparc32/xtensa.

**Boot-time entropy.** **Heninger-Durumeric-Wustrow-Halderman**, *Mining Your Ps and Qs* (USENIX Security 2012, Best Paper; 2022 Test-of-Time) [50], scanned 22M hosts and factored ~24,000 TLS RSA keys and computed ~1,000 SSH DSA keys via batch-GCD because Linux key-generation occurred before the entropy pool had filled. **Lenstra-Hughes-Augier-Bos-Kleinjung-Wachter**, *Ron was wrong, Whit is right* (Crypto 2012, ePrint 2012/064) [51], independently found ~0.2% of Internet RSA moduli share primes. **Hastings-Fried-Heninger**, *Weak Keys Remain Widespread in Network Devices* (IMC 2016) [52], found the rate **essentially unchanged** four years later (~0.37% factored). **Bernstein-Chang-Cheng-Chou-Heninger-Lange-van Someren**, *Factoring RSA keys from certified smart cards: Coppersmith in the wild* (Asiacrypt 2013, ePrint 2013/599) [53], factored 184 keys from FIPS 140-2 Level 2-certified Taiwanese citizen ID cards.

**Windows.** **Dorrendorf-Gutterman-Pinkas**, *Cryptanalysis of the Windows Random Number Generator* (CCS 2007, ACM TISSEC 2009) [54], reverse-engineered Windows 2000 CryptGenRandom and presented an O(2^23) forward-state attack. No public top-tier cryptanalysis of modern Windows 10/11 BCryptGenRandom CNG was found.

**Virtualization.** **Ristenpart & Yilek**, *When Good Randomness Goes Bad: VM Reset Vulnerabilities* (NDSS 2010) [55], demonstrated snapshot-rollback nonce/PRNG reuse breaking Apache+OpenSSL TLS and exposing DSA signing keys; proposed hedged cryptography. **Everspaugh-Zhai-Jellinek-Ristenpart-Swift**, *Not-So-Random Numbers in Virtualized Linux and the Whirlwind RNG* (IEEE S&P 2014) [56], showed Xen, VMware, and EC2 VMs emit identical RNG outputs from identical snapshots and that virtualized disk timing yields negligible entropy.

**Android/iOS.** The Android SecureRandom bug (2013), disclosed by **Klyubin** on the Android Developers Blog [57] and analyzed in **Kim-Han-Lee**, *Predictability of Android OpenSSL's PRNG* (CCS 2013) [58], caused Bitcoin wallet ECDSA nonces to repeat across signatures (Apache Harmony SHA1PRNG could be uninitialized in some code paths), enabling lattice key recovery and ~55 BTC in losses.

**Hardware-RNG trust into OS pools.** Post-Snowden 2013, the Linux community rejected RDRAND-only entropy (Torvalds, Ts'o) [59]; FreeBSD 10 removed direct RDRAND/Padlock output. **Garfinkel & Leclerc** (WPES 2020) [60] document the US Census Bureau's policy of mixing /dev/urandom with RDSEED for the same reason. **CrossTalk** (above) provides the empirical justification.

## Layer 3: cryptographic entropy, broken at the algorithm and standard

**Dual_EC_DRBG.** **Shumow & Ferguson** (Crypto 2007 Rump) [61] demonstrated the kleptographic trapdoor in the SP 800-90 P,Q parameters: ~32 bytes of output reveal future state to anyone knowing the secret d with P = dQ. **Checkoway et al.**, *On the Practical Exploitability of Dual EC in TLS Implementations* (USENIX Security 2014) [62], gave concrete attack costs against BSAFE-C, BSAFE-Java, SChannel, and OpenSSL-FIPS, finding the 2007 SP 800-90 revision (longer truncation) made exploitation *easier*. **Checkoway et al.**, *A Systematic Analysis of the Juniper Dual EC Incident* (CCS 2016, CVE-2015-7755/7756) [63], reverse-engineered Juniper ScreenOS and demonstrated passive VPN decryption on real NetScreen hardware. **Bernstein-Lange-Niederhagen**, *Dual EC: A Standardized Back Door* (Festschrift Kahn, LNCS 9100, 2016, ePrint 2015/767) [64], documents the full standards-process history and Certicom patent record. NIST removed Dual_EC_DRBG in SP 800-90A Rev. 1 (June 2015) [65].

**Debian OpenSSL CVE-2008-0166.** A Valgrind-quieting patch removed entropy mixing from OpenSSL's PRNG, collapsing seed entropy to PID space (≤32,768 values) [66]. All Debian/Ubuntu-generated SSH/OpenVPN/DNSSEC/X.509 keys (Sep 2006–May 2008) were brute-forceable [67]. **Yilek-Rescorla-Shacham-Enright-Savage**, *When Private Keys are Public* (IMC 2009) [68], measured the slow remediation curve — 30% of vulnerable hosts remained vulnerable six months after disclosure.

**ECDSA nonce reuse and biased nonces.** **fail0verflow**'s 27C3 talk (Dec 2010) [69] showed Sony's PS3 firmware-signing reused a *constant* k across all signatures; (m₁ − m₂)/(s₁ − s₂) yields k and then d. **Howgrave-Graham & Smart** (Des. Codes Cryptogr. 2001) and **Nguyen & Shparlinski** (J. Cryptology 2002) [70] gave the rigorous Hidden-Number-Problem lattice attacks; **Bleichenbacher**'s unpublished P1363 work (2000) on biased nonces was rediscovered in **De Mulder et al.** (CHES 2013) [71]. The recent practical breaks form a chain: **Jancar-Sedlacek-Svenda-Sys**, *Minerva* (TCHES 2020/4, Best Paper) [72] — 500–2,100 signatures recover ECDSA keys from FIPS-certified Atmel AT90SC smartcards and libgcrypt/wolfSSL/MatrixSSL/SunEC/Crypto++; **Moghimi-Sunar-Eisenbarth-Heninger**, *TPM-FAIL* (USENIX Security 2020) [73] — ~1,300 traces recover 256-bit ECDSA from Intel fTPM, with a remote attack on StrongSwan IPsec/VPN; **Aranha-Novaes-Takahashi-Tibouchi-Yarom**, *LadderLeak* (CCS 2020) [74] — breaks ECDSA with <1 bit of nonce leakage in real OpenSSL Montgomery-ladder code. **Breitner & Heninger**, *Biased Nonce Sense* (FC 2019, ePrint 2019/023) [75], mined blockchains and Internet-wide scans to recover ~300 Bitcoin private keys and many SSH/HTTPS keys from biased nonces in the wild.

**IV/nonce reuse in symmetric crypto.** **Joux**'s "forbidden attack" (NIST GCM comment, 2006) [76] recovers the GHASH authentication subkey from a single repeated (key, nonce). **Böck-Zauner-Devlin-Somorovsky-Jovanovic**, *Nonce-Disrespecting Adversaries* (USENIX WOOT 2016) [77], Internet-scanned 184 HTTPS servers reusing GCM nonces and triggered CVE-2016-0270 (IBM Domino), CVE-2016-10212 (Radware), CVE-2017-5933 (Citrix NetScaler). **Vanhoef & Piessens**, *KRACK* (CCS 2017, CVE-2017-13077..13088) [78], forces nonce reuse in WPA2's 4-way handshake by replaying message 3; with AES-CCMP this enables decryption/replay, with GCMP it enables forgery, and wpa_supplicant 2.4/2.5 catastrophically installs an all-zero key. **Fluhrer-Mantin-Shamir** (SAC 2001) [79] on WEP, plus **Klein** (DCC 2008) and **Tews-Weinmann-Pyshkin**, *Breaking 104-bit WEP in Less than 60 Seconds* (WISA 2007) [80], killed WEP by exploiting IV-key concatenation in RC4's KSA.

**DRBG state compromise.** **Cohney-Green-Heninger**, *Practical State Recovery Attacks against Legacy RNG Implementations* (DUHK, CCS 2018) [81], found 12 FIPS 140-2 certified products (including Fortinet FortiGate, BeCrypt, Cisco) shipping with **hard-coded ANSI X9.31 keys** — a flaw known since Kelsey-Schneier-Wagner-Hall (FSE 1998). Authors built a working passive VPN-decryption attack on ~25,000 FortiGate devices. **Woodage & Shumow**, *An Analysis of the NIST SP 800-90A Standard* (Eurocrypt 2019, ePrint 2018/349) [82], gave the first rigorous analysis under the DPRVW robustness model: HMAC-DRBG without additional_input is not forward-secure, and CTR-DRBG's spec flexibility admits insecure OpenSSL configurations. **Hoang & Shen** (Crypto 2020, ePrint 2020/619) [83] repaired Hutchinson's earlier CTR-DRBG security proof. **Cohney-Kwong-Paz-Genkin-Heninger-Ronen-Yarom**, *Pseudorandom Black Swans: Cache Attacks on CTR_DRBG* (IEEE S&P 2020) [84], shows CTR_DRBG lacks forward security under side-channel state compromise (OpenSSL FIPS, NetBSD kernel, SGX in three AES encryptions). **Michaelis-Meyer-Schwenk**, *Randomly Failed!* (CT-RSA 2013) [85], audited Java implementations and uncovered the Apache Harmony SHA1PRNG bug later weaponized against Android Bitcoin wallets.

**Standards.** Beyond Dual_EC, **Killmann & Schindler**'s BSI AIS 20/31 (v3.0, Sept 2024) [86] requires a *stochastic model* of the entropy source plus continuous online tests — the canonical European alternative to NIST SP 800-90B's pure min-entropy estimation. **ROCA (CVE-2017-15361)** by **Nemec-Sys-Svenda-Klinec-Matyas** (CCS 2017) [87] factored 1024-bit RSA in 97 CPU-days from Infineon RSALib's structured primes (p = k·M + 65537ᵃ mod M) — affecting FIPS 140-2 and CC EAL 5+ certified TPMs, YubiKey 4, and 750,000 Estonian national ID cards. Minerva and TPM-FAIL (above) likewise exposed Common Criteria EAL 4+ failures. **Argyros & Kiayias**, *I Forgot Your Password: Randomness Attacks Against PHP Applications* (USENIX Security 2012) [88], took over Mediawiki/Joomla/Gallery/osCommerce/HotCRP accounts via predictable mt_rand/lcg_value/uniqid-based password-reset tokens.

|---|---|---|
| Shannon (malware detection) | Mantovani et al., NDSS 2020 | Application evadable |

## Conclusion: the three-fold diagnosis

Reading this corpus in aggregate reveals a simple diagnostic. **First**, no commonly used mathematical entropy measure has been "broken" in the sense that the definition is wrong; what is broken is the *modeling decision* to use Shannon entropy where min-entropy is the operational requirement, and that mistake recurs from password policies (NIST SP800-63) to quantitative information flow (Shannon-MI) to malware detection thresholds. **Second**, the NIST SP 800-90B estimator suite is structurally flawed: estimators are correlated (so "take the min" doesn't protect), some over- and some under-estimate, and the LRS estimator measures collision entropy while being treated as a min-entropy estimator. AIS 20/31's stochastic-model requirement is the cleaner answer, but it is also harder to comply with. **Third**, the catastrophic field failures — Debian, Mining Ps and Qs, PS3, KRACK, ROCA, DUHK, Minerva, TPM-FAIL, Juniper Dual_EC — were not measure failures or estimator failures: they were failures to deliver *any* entropy where uniform randomness was assumed. That repetition, across 18 years and every layer of the stack, is the real lesson.

The actionable takeaway for security engineers is to **always state which entropy measure** is being claimed (Shannon vs min vs collision), **always state which layer** the claim lives at (silicon, OS, algorithm), and **always demand a robust extractor** between them. The DPRVW robustness model, BSI AIS 20/31's stochastic-model + online-test discipline, and the post-Snowden community norm of mixing rather than trusting opaque hardware RNGs are the engineering crystallization of that lesson. Anything claiming "high entropy" without specifying these three is, in the light of this literature, almost certainly broken — we just may not have read the right paper yet.

## Find out what happens when your entropy fails

The literature above tells you *that* entropy claims break; these tools let you watch it happen on your own data and apply the actual standardized test batteries. Run them against a source you suspect, against `/dev/urandom`, and against a deliberately broken generator (a fixed seed, a biased coin, a low-period LCG) — the contrast is the fastest way to build intuition for everything in this post.

- **AIS 31 full evaluation** — <https://github.com/owlmt/ais31-full-evaluation> — implements the BSI AIS 20/31 test procedures (the stochastic-model + online-test discipline discussed above). Use this when you want the *European* style of evaluation that demands a model of the source, not just a pass/fail on the output.
- **NIST uniformity demo** — <https://github.com/owlmt/nist-uniformity-demo> — demonstrates the NIST-style uniformity / SP 800-22-family checks. Pair this with the caution from Hurley-Smith & Hernandez-Castro (TOPS 2020): biased QRNG streams *passed* SP 800-22 while failing Dieharder/TestU01, so treat a green result here as necessary, never sufficient.
- **RNG uniformity demo** — <https://github.com/owlmt/rng-uniformity-demo> — a lighter-weight uniformity visualizer for quickly eyeballing distribution shape, bias, and short-period artifacts before reaching for the heavier batteries.

A practical workflow: estimate min-entropy first (not Shannon), then run AIS 31 for the model-based view and the NIST/uniformity demos for the output-distribution view — and remember that *every* failure in the table above eventually surfaced as either a bad min-entropy estimate or a source producing structured output a uniformity test could have flagged, if anyone had run one.
